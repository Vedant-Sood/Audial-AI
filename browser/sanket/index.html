        Tone cues

        Cues describe what a voice did, never what someone feels. Reading the person stays with you.

        Tone estimates

        Sanket compares each voice with how that person usually sounds, after hearing them a few times. It notes “more intense than usual” or “softer, sad-sounding”, and names an emotion only when it is at least 80% sure. These are estimates of how a voice sounds, learned from actors, not readings of what anyone feels.

            How accurate are tone estimates?

            Sanket learned tone from CREMA-D: 7,441 short sentences by 91 actors, each acting six emotions. It was tested on 10 actors it never heard in training, using the speaker split from Audial AI, and on natural, unacted reading. “Was” is Sanket’s earlier loudness-and-pitch rules on the same test.

            Tested on 10 actors Sanket never heardResult
            Best guess among six acted emotions (chance: 17%)56%
            Emotion named (≥ 80% sure): how often right98%
            …and how often Sanket is that sure4% of sentences
            “Voice rose” on angry, happy or fearful speech50% (was 41%)
            “Voice rose” wrongly, on neutral speech2% (was 12%)
            “Voice softened” on sad speech43% (was 28%)
            “Voice softened” wrongly, on angry, happy or fearful speech6% (was 12%)

            On 4 minutes of natural, calm reading by three other people, Sanket named an emotion once in 55 stretches (wrongly: “happy”) and gave no tone cues.

            Limits. The actors are American and the emotions are acted. Sanket has not yet been tested on Indian English or Hindi speakers, or on real classroom conversation. Treat every estimate as a hint to look again, not a fact about how someone feels. Sanket needs to hear a person a few times before it estimates anything about them.
