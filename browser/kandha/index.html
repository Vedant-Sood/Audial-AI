              Tone estimates

              Kandha compares each voice with how that person usually sounds, after hearing them a few times. It notes "more intense than usual" or "softer, sad-sounding", and names an emotion only when it is at least 80% sure. These are estimates of how a voice sounds, learned from actors, not readings of what anyone feels.

                How accurate are tone estimates?

                Kandha's tone model learned from CREMA-D: 7,441 short sentences by 91 actors, each acting six emotions. It was tested on 10 actors it never heard in training, using the speaker split from Audial-AI, and on natural, unacted reading. "Was" is the earlier loudness-and-pitch rules on the same test.

                Tested on 10 actors the model never heardResult
                Best guess among six acted emotions (chance: 17%)56%
                Emotion named (≥ 80% sure): how often right98%
                …and how often it is that sure4% of sentences
                "Voice rose" on angry, happy or fearful speech50% (was 41%)
                "Voice rose" wrongly, on neutral speech2% (was 12%)
                "Voice softened" on sad speech43% (was 28%)
                "Voice softened" wrongly, on angry, happy or fearful speech6% (was 12%)

                On 4 minutes of natural, calm reading by three other people, the model named an emotion once in 55 stretches (wrongly: "happy") and gave no tone cues. Speaker colours in a stitched four-person seminar were right 98% of the time.

                Limits. The actors are American and the emotions are acted. Nothing has been tested on Indian English or Hindi speakers, on real classroom conversation, or with a physical stole. Treat every estimate as a hint to look again, not a fact about how someone feels. Kandha needs to hear a person a few times before it estimates anything about them.
